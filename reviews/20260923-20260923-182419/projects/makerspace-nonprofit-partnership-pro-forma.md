---# Makerspace — Nonprofit Partnership & Pro-Forma (v1)

Base market: Columbus, OH (ranked #1 in state analysis). All numbers are model assumptions — adjustable.

### 1. Partnership strategy (how to get 501(c)(3) umbrella)

**Key constraint:** the big grant pools (EDA $500k–$10M+, USDA RBDG $500k, ARC, CDBG) require a nonprofit or government applicant. A for-profit LLC can't touch them. Solution: layered partnership, in order.

#### Option A — Community college / workforce nonprofit (BEST)
- Already tax-exempt, has facility, and its mission (workforce training) is the #1 fundable outcome for EDA/USDA/state grants
- Real examples: Mayland Community College Enterprise Corp (NC) runs a 13,000 SF advanced-manufacturing makerspace funded by Duke Energy ($250k) + Golden LEAF; CA CCC Maker funded 24 colleges at $100k–$350k/yr; Houston makerspace won $5M EDA through a nonprofit
- Pitch: they get a training pipeline + grant administration; you get the building + tax status

#### Option B — County/City EDC (BEST for capital buildout)
- EDCs are the natural applicants for EDA Public Works, CDBG, ARC, state incentive money
- Example: Sheboygan County EDC funded a makerspace feasibility study; PA DCED highlights funded makerspaces
- Pitch: job creation, downtown revitalization, local supply-chain retention

#### Option C — Fiscal sponsorship (FASTEST to launch, use for months 0–24)
- Model C (pre-approved grants, project stays independent): fees 4–10%
- Model A (sponsor runs back office): fees 9–15%
- Where to find: fiscalsponsordirectory.org, Unleash Your Ideas directory (300+ sponsors), local community foundation
- Best fits: community-development sponsors (Urban Affairs Coalition Philly, New Sun Rising Pittsburgh, IncludUs) or your regional community foundation; Fractured Atlas is arts-focused (less ideal)
- Spin out into your own 501(c)(3) once revenue exceeds ~$250k–$500k

#### Option D — Own 501(c)(3) (LONG-TERM)
- Cost: $275–$600 IRS filing + $1k–$5k legal + 3–12 months wait; then board, bylaws, 990s, D&O insurance
- Take this route once sponsored revenue justifies it (see Scenario table)

#### Timeline
- Months 0–2: fiscal sponsor agreement + founding board + feasibility data
- Months 0–6: community college/EDC MOU + first grant applications (state fast-track)
- Months 6–12: operate under sponsor; apply EDA/USDA/ARC (capital) via partner
- Months 18–36: spin out own 501(c)(3)

### 5. Scenarios & break-even

| Scenario | Members | Earned revenue | Annual cost | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 90 | ~$95k | $100k lean | −$5k (needs ~$25k grant) |
| Base | 170 | ~$162k | $154k staffed | +$8k, self-sustaining |
| Stretch | 260 | ~$240k | $219k staffed + FT director | +$21k surplus → spin off 501(c)(3) |

Break-even dues math: at ~$50/mo average, dues ≈ $8,300/mo at 170 members; plus ~$1,200/mo non-dues = ~$9,500/mo vs $8,330/mo lean. Self-sustaining at ~150–170 members volunteer-led; ~200–230 members (or strong program revenue) to add paid staff.

Market check: 170 members = ~0.008% of Columbus metro (2.2M). Comparable mature spaces in similar metros run 250–600 members — target is conservative.

### 6. Cash & risk rules
- Hold 3 months lean opex ($25–30k) minimum before launch
- Startup needs ~$100k+ runway on top of capital
- Insurance is the gating risk: under-18/volunteer policies, equipment certifications, liability waivers
- 2026 industrial market is tenant-friendly: negotiate free rent + TI allowance on 5-yr lease

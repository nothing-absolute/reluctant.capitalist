## The Off-Ramp — YouTube Channel Plan

### One-Liner
> The off-ramp from subscription tech: free, open-source AI and computers you don't have to replace — tested honestly on hardware normal people own.

### Mission / Angle
Help average computer users (normies, all ages) see how open-source tools can replace paid subscription AI models — save money, avoid corporate walled gardens, and extend the life of hardware they already own.

**KEY: This is NOT aimed at the FOSS audience.** The goal is bringing new users *into* awareness of FOSS options. The FOSS community serves itself; this channel is the bridge/on-ramp from the mainstream.

- Benefits-first language, zero jargon
- "You can do X without paying" — never "here is tool Y built on framework Z"
- Never scolding, always evoking: you don't *need* to keep paying, you don't *need* to keep upgrading
- **LLM as tech support (flagship)**: show normies how a free/local LLM replaces endless web searching and blog reading. It audits their current computer + software + needs, then outputs a clear, non-jargon migration plan. The model is the tech-support desk; the channel is the guide.
- The "Switch" journey is the flagship series: audit what you have → state what you want to do → get a simple step-by-step path — per user type, starting from absolute zero.
- **Brand structure:** The Off-Ramp is the channel + store brand. "The $0 Swap" is the flagship **series** within it — every comparison video is "another $0 Swap," and the public rerunnable benchmarks are "The $0 Swap Benchmarks."

### Why This Gap Exists (validation, Sept 2026)
- Existing "local AI" YouTube is developer-shaped: 111 creators, 200+ indexed Ollama videos, mostly terminal/coding content (top video: 588K views, "Claude Code FREE Forever via Ollama")
- Closest normie-angled channel: The Art of The Terminal (~7.8K subs) — tiny
- Ollama raised a $65M Series B (2026) — mainstream gravity with no mainstream explainer
- Open models now 6-12 months behind frontier (was 2+ years in 2023); DeepSeek Coder beats GPT-4 on HumanEval
- "Free ≠ private" conflation is rampant in this space — an accuracy-first channel can own honesty

### Audience
- Currently paying $10-200/mo across ChatGPT, Midjourney, Otter.ai, Descript, HeyGen, Grammarly, etc.
- Buying new laptops because the old one is "slow"
- Caring about: money, privacy, corporate tracking, feeling trapped by subscriptions
- NOT: developers, self-hosters, FOSS enthusiasts (already served elsewhere)

### Non-Slop Moat (4 Rules)
1. **Live side-by-side benchmarks** — same prompt through the paid and free tool, on screen, unedited. Verifiable > claim.
2. **Honest hardware** — test on a normie rig (8-16GB RAM laptop), not a 4090. "Runs on: any computer from the last 5 years."
3. **"Free ≠ private" honesty rule** — flag tools marketed as local that quietly phone home (e.g., MoneyPrinterTurbo's default Edge TTS → Microsoft cloud). Making this distinction is the trust signature.
4. **Right-size expectations** — small local models nail everyday writing/email/transcription, not every task. Tell people *when* free is great and when it isn't. That honesty is the brand.

#### Pillar F rationale (non-AI expansion)
- Extends the money/independence narrative beyond AI → broader runway, second revenue surface
- Install Linux (Mint/Zorin/Ubuntu) on slow Macs/PCs — most "obsolete" machines are fine with a lightweight OS
- Pairs beautifully with AI content: old laptop + Linux + small local models = literally keep what you have
- Viral-history proof: "Linux on old MacBook" videos get millions of views; the normie spin ("the upgrade cycle is a scam") is the fresh angle
- Unlocks hardware-adjacent affiliate: used desktops, Mini PCs, RAM

#### Pillar G rationale — The Switch (the fusion flagship)
- **The model is the tech-support desk, the channel is the guide.** Normies currently face hours of hunting across blogs, forums, and videos to figure out a migration. A free/local LLM collapses that: it gathers specs and software inventory (the model tells them the exact steps to run), maps needs/goals, and produces one clear non-jargon plan.
- Demonstrates **both pillars at once** — AI powering the Linux/FOSS story. Nothing else on YouTube does "LLM-assisted migration planning."
- Deeply repeatable series: start at absolute zero, one user archetype per episode (browser-and-email senior, home-office worker, local-AI/creator), each with a new-to-it viewer.
- **Funnel engine**: the audit workflow (script template) is a lead magnet and digital product; each machine build and each archetype plan is content that pre-sells the hardware store.

### Phased Roadmap — Views first, revenue later
**Why this order:** Block One is the view engine — fastest to produce (screen recordings + voiceover, no hardware builds), highest search intent ("free ChatGPT alternative", "how to cancel [tool]"), and it builds the anti-slop workflow and trust. Block Two reuses that trust and those editing templates to branch into hardware/Linux/migration — harder to make, but it's what turns viewers into subscribers and eventually customers. Block Three monetizes what the first two built. Everything else in this doc (store, products, sponsors, extra pillars) slots into Block Three or later.

### Monetization
- AdSense (primary): blended RPM $5-12 (US-skewing, purchase-intent audience)
- Affiliate: used desktops/Mini PCs, RAM, hosting (Hetzner), GPU rentals (Runpod lane)
- Digital product: "The FOSS Liberation Kit" — a tested, scripted swap guide
- **"The Switch Audit"** — the reusable LLM prompt template + checklist the series is built on, sold as the step-one artifact (audit your machine → get your plan)
- Sponsors: distro, hardware, hosting vendors
- Community: Liberapay/Ko-fi — this audience donates; it hates subscriptions

#### Hardware store — the done-for-you tier
- Shopify-style store: refurbished/used hardware with Linux **pre-installed**, for viewers with limited time or patience for DIY. The off-ramp when the series convinces them the switch is worth it.
- Three models, one per end-user archetype:
  1. **The Life Browser** — cheapest old laptop/desktop, Mint/Zorin, email + browsing + video (~$100-200)
  2. **The Home Office** — Mini PC or used laptop, 16GB RAM, LibreOffice + small local LLM built in (~$250-400)
  3. **The Local Creator** — used GPU workstation, ComfyUI + Ollama ready out of the box (~$500-800)
- **Every machine is content**: each tier gets a "watch me build THIS model" video and an archetype Switch episode — the store is pre-sold by the channel, not advertised cold.
- Path: validate demand with a waitlist/pre-sell and affiliate deals with existing Linux-refurb sellers *before* holding any inventory. Own inventory only once orders justify it.
- Honesty rule applies here too: be transparent about specs, age, cooling, and what each tier can't do.

### Revenue Timeline (phased)
- Block One (m1-3): $0. Goal is views + subs + a dialed-in workflow. Monetization threshold not reached.
- Block Two (m3-6): cross 1,000 subs + 4,000 watch-hrs, AdSense switches on, $200-800/mo
- Block Three (m6-12): 40-60 videos compounding, $800-3,000/mo — **$1,000+/mo achievable within the year** via RPM $5-12 + affiliate + first product launches
- Month 12+: brand deals + The Switch Audit + hardware store all feeding in; AdSense is the floor, not the ceiling
- Affiliate links woven in from Block One (used Mini PCs, RAM, hosting, Runpod); store is the outsized upside ($100-800/unit)

### Risks
1. **Accuracy burden** — value claims must be reproducible. Mitigated by the live-test format.
2. **Tool churn** — local AI moves monthly. Mitigated: public, dated benchmark doc that becomes a reference asset.
3. **Jargon creep (main risk for a normie channel)** — every video must be understandable to a 60-year-old. FOSS-slang and gatekeeping = channel death. Keep it benefits-first, always.
4. **Monetization skepticism** — far lower for this audience than for FOSS-hardliners (normies expect creators to earn), but stay transparent anyway.
5. **Hardware variance** — "runs on any computer" claims must be tested. Mitigated: state the tested hardware floor explicitly in every video.

### Community Data Center / Third-Space Cooperative — Vision (JD, 2026)

The long dream (surfaced fully in the 2026-09-19 Claude session): every city/region gets a **locally owned data center + compute commons** inside a dead mall or unused industrial building, adjacent to a **mini modular factory / upcycle center** — 3D printing and modern modular manufacturing — plus the full third-space stack. Not utopia; a legit alternative operating system with working historical precedent.

#### The Stack (one site)
- Local data center / compute layer — the critical missing piece nobody has attempted at scale. Compute is the new landlord; community ownership of GPUs flips the power dynamic.
- Mini modular factory / upcycle center: 3D printers, automated clothing manufacturing/customization, electronics recycling/refurbishment.
- Computer labs, printing, open industrial kitchen, artist + musician studios, coworking, education + mentoring.
- The hacker/makerspace at the core — resurrection of the third space.

#### Precedents already in the game
- **Bell Labs / ARPANET / land-grant universities** — public/patron-funded research whose output belonged to humanity.
- **Early hackerspaces**: Chaos Computer Club, MIT AI Lab — community-governed, enormous value.
- **FabCity network** — Barcelona/Amsterdam distributed local manufacturing experiments (already on the radar as model + warning).
- **Mondragon** — worker-owned, decades long, but made compromises.
- **Free Geek Portland** — opened April 22, 2000 (Earth Day), Oso Martin, Pioneer Courthouse Square → SE 10th; consensus-based volunteer governance that drifted to nonprofit-ED model as it grew — the classic cooperative scaling failure mode to design against. Free Geek Twin Cities opened 2009 (South Mpls).
- **Precious Plastic (Dave Hakkens)** — the origin inspiration (~5 yrs ago): modular, low-tech, open-hardware plastic-bottle recycling machines; wiki-as-factory. Proof that open hardware docs + distributed community can replicate manufacturing without corporate structure.
- **Gopher (U. Minnesota)** — the cautionary tale: nearly dominant internet protocol; university slapped licensing fees on it and the community walked to the open alternative within a year. Enclosing a commons kills it. Freedom beats convenience when the alternative is genuinely better.
- **Earthship** — the build-anywhere lesson: proven, documented tornado/hurricane-resistant homes from trash that cost ~nothing to heat/cool, blocked not by engineering but by zoning codes written for the conventional construction industry. Regulatory capture, not physics.

#### Governance & Funding (deliberately no patron)
- Structured as a **cooperative**, no leaders, no charismatic-founder capture, no single big funder (e.g., an Intel that would steer electronics design).
- Not the "Bill & Melinda Gates–funded incubator feeding private capital lizards." The point is to not become a new funnel for extractive capital.
- Funding: community land trusts, solidarity-economy networks, **worker cooperative loan funds (e.g., Shared Capital Cooperative)**, near-zero long-term leases on dead malls (municipalities desperate for anything not-a-liability), and $20–100/mo member subscriptions — distributed buy-in keeps governance distributed. Mondragon started this way.
- **"Physical GPL"** — anything developed using the space/infrastructure must open what it built (license framework for physical goods; legally messy, worth solving).
- The first 50 people who show up determine what it becomes. Bootstrapping culture, not tech, is the hard problem: retired machinist + teenager + single mom + programmer in one room, or it's just another tech-bro makerspace.

#### Why it's the "better business model" now
- Shipping bits of cloth/plastic/buttons/containers around the world to make cheap things is obsolete; distributed local manufacturing + personal fabrication is cheaper, skilled, and community-building.
- Amazon/Walmart purchases are largely manufacturable locally, saving people money and providing skills/education.
- Open source, modular, always upgradeable/repairable; cooperation across sectors and disciplines; no middle management, no C-suite, no bullshit jobs. Not "bring manufacturing back to America" nationalism — it's opening access.
- Not-for-profit for community/co-op benefit in whatever form that takes (education, housing, energy, transport).

#### Education piece
- K-12 was designed to make factory/compliance workers (bells, rows, standardized output, age segregation, subject siloing).
- The alternative: mixed-age apprenticeship — a 14-year-old learning electronics by building alongside a 45-year-old engineer. How humans learned for most of history.
- Kids don't need the public-school factory model; they need projects, tools, and mentors.

#### The wider thesis
- Not political, not national, not religious, not exclusionary — **humanistic and cooperative**. Explicitly political movements get captured; something framed on practical needs (fix your laptop, learn to make things, grow food, get online) is hard to demonize or co-opt. The person who comes to fix their laptop doesn't need to be an anarchist.
- "We have no real vision of how to escape capitalism" → the vision exists now, and it's buildable with today's tools (open hardware, cooperative law, empty malls, cheap compute).
- Solarpunk isn't a genre fantasy; it's the better business model for the modern era.

#### Open questions
- How the network of independent centers shares innovations without redundant work (open comms between co-ops, interstate rail for people+distribution, etc.).
- Legal architecture for physical GPL / open-what-you-build.
- Governance guardrails against the Free Geek drift (consensus → ED capture) at scale.
- Initial capitalization without a patron while staying unattached (see funding above).

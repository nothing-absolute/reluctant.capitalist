Started as an exercise in campaign craftsmanship: can a mundane hardware product — something to hold a door open — be wrapped in a story good enough to fund on Kickstarter?

Built a full mock landing page (`opendoor-kickstarter-mock.html`) with:

- Reward tier structure with fully-loaded cost math (product + shipping + platform fees + contingency)
- Kickstarter fee modeling: 5% platform + ~3–5% payment processing
- Prosumer positioning contrast: why this is a "prosumer" product, not a commodity doorstop

**Status:** concept — the mock exists, the product does not. The real output was the discipline of pricing a physical good honestly.

## The numbers that taught me something

| Tier | Price | COGS | Margin after fees |
|------|-------|------|-------------------|
| Single | $29 | ~$9 | ~55% |
| Family | $49 | ~$17 | ~58% |

Kickstarter success rates for hardware are brutal; the campaign math only works with 1,000+ pre-launch emails. Lesson: the audience is the product, the campaign is the distribution event.

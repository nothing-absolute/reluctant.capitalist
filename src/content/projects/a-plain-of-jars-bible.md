A Plain of Jars — Project Bible

V1 capture of the 2026-09-19 Claude conversation (full live session). Working title: *A Plain of Jars* (open to change). Form: **3-volume literary memoir graphic novel**, with animation/cartoon adaptation potential. Substack serialization + podcast as release/build channels.

Thesis
A hyperreal-recall narrator ("future JD") slips back and forth through his own past — the mechanism is not a sci-fi device, it *is* how the narrator's mind genuinely works. The story carries arguments about class, information access, systems failure, addiction treatment, cooperative futures, and what it meant to come of age at a specific cultural inflection point (Sioux Falls, 1984-born, straight line from ska basements to the internet).

Three-Act / Three-Volume Structure

Volume 1 — Act One: Formation (Sioux Falls, ~1988–2004, then the move west) — ~150–180 pp

- Age 4: Dad explains the allowance split (books / charity / savings). "I'M 4, you're an asshole."
- ACE Elementary (All City) — alternative public school, diverse class, involved parents, computers. The good version of school. OJ Bronco chase wheeled in on a TV at lunch ("Why? We're kids?"). Best friend's dad comes out, 5th grade.
- Slammed into public middle school. Almost lost it. GATE program. **RAND corporation scantrons** quarterly — drug knowledge/use surveys, never explained, continue through graduation.
- **Spencer**: first new middle-school friend, Marilyn Manson shirt (pre-MTV Florida era). Dad tries to ban him. JD doesn't listen. Spencer introduces **Skankin' Pickle + Asian Man Records**. The fork in the road — if dad had won that fight, none of this exists.
- Music scene: ska at 13 → punk → Saddle Creek (Omaha, 3.5 hrs) → basement show circuit (Sioux Falls on the MIN → OMA → DEN touring corridor). First show: The Faint, basement, 15–20 people. Life-changing.
- Working at 14 (legal in SD) → buys guitar gear.
- **A Plain of Jars**: instrumental band — JD (guitar), Nicole (guitar), Andrew (drums). Recorded an album JD is still proud of; ~10 shows; breaks up on graduation.
- **Tut Hill Park** disc golf course: tree-to-tree, passed down kid-to-kid for years, metal baskets later installed almost exactly on the old course. Culture as recursion.
- **Corey**: rich psycho friend. Clothesline strung at neck height across his block on JD's paper route. Kicks the basement window out at a sleepover ("I was trying to knock"). Parents: former hippies, rich, don't seem to work, kept the '60s VW wagon, moved to Utah, Olympic tickets lying around, UK boarding school for Corey's senior year. CIA-adjacent in texture, not conspiracy.
- **Amsterdam** (~age 19): budget student fare, hostel over a canal, alleys thick with offers. Legit mushroom store, helpful staff, separate packages for JD and Jeff. JD feels suddenly, weirdly tired after his dose → blank → smash cut → fully pinned to the cot, hours, can't move, can barely talk. Corey in and out. Jeff returns: "she put the condom on with her mouth dude!!!" (Almost certainly a mushroom swap — high dose given to JD, who wanted to stretch his supply across three nights.)
- First sex with Nicole (junior year) on oxy — lasts far longer than it should for a first time. Same year Dick Cheney shot that guy in the face; Nicole's dad also shot on a hunting trip that same year.
- Dad throughline: Bush Sr. rally; angry Christians smashing cement blocks in an arena (JD goes up on stage, does NOT pledge — agnostic since ~10, just lacked the word); Sam's Club gumball machine ("I don't know who Sam is…"); Toastmasters / MLM / The Secret / NLP.
- Weird texture: Erowid from ~15 (made him a level-headed user, not an addict — limits set young: no meth, no needles); bootleg Grateful Dead/Phish tape trading around the world, gift economy, no money.
- **Act One curtain**: leaving for the Art Institute of Seattle. Nicole's frisbee game in Seattle; they reconnect and make it work through the degree. Audio engineering; podcasting at end of school ("that will never make any money" — 2009, ha).

Volume 2 — Act Two: Collapse (Seattle / Portland / the tunnel, ~2005–2012) — ~120–150 pp

Visually non-linear, dark pages, fragmented panels, time compression. The tunnel isn't just a narrative problem — it's where the time mechanics begin; formative psychedelics were the training ground.

- Portland with Nicole. Lewis & Clark. Class tension runs under a genuine relationship. The glass house: ultra-modern, down a hidden forested driveway, ~5–10 min from downtown, set in a lush reserve. Longboarding the winding road to it. Full-page spread candidate, no dialogue.
- **Andrew dies** — cystic fibrosis meant he was always going to die young, but it was an opiate OD. First clinical depression; no light at all, first time. Vodka on the sly at the pizza delivery job; everyone smoked weed at work, but vodka wasn't the move. **Wellbutrin** pulls him back. (Dad: "didn't believe in therapy." ok cool dad.)
- **OPB pledge drive grand prize**: Rick Steves complete paid Europe tour. Won while in the grief hole; found with **2 days left** on a 30-day claim window in the mail pile.
- **Marbella, Spain**: Nicole's parents' Disney-points apartment, one week. Dope sick the first 4 days — the gap between the life being offered and where he actually was, made physical. Then Paris train + Rick Steves PBS tour package (wholesome container for emotional wreckage). The relationship fizzles — no blowout, a quiet knowing that the opiates had him and Nicole wasn't up for it.
- **Rick Steves as character**: fictionalized as the tour guide — the voice of the mainstream narrative JD is quietly departing, and the road-not-taken made flesh (the OPB life was a real open door: degree, the medium, early podcasting, ~2009).
- **Adam**: Seattle complex, 2 floors up. Day two: "dude I got heroin." Wealthy family, disposed to addiction. Prostitutes living in his studio to score. A sociopathic scammer latched on, placating JD while extracting. Parents take him to rehab; JD cleans the place — bags of garbage, needles everywhere, **gets poked** on a bag. Adam dies ~2 years after Andrew.
- **15 people JD knew die of OD from opiates** — "the whole sentence" is that count vs. the narrator being alive to tell it. Not chapter-by-chapter.
- Years of heavy oxy funded by reliably selling weed. Two bands — the best-band-years; records, a few tours. Bills stop. Switch to heroin. The roommate led astray — told him it was opium, they smoked it together; one of the only things JD genuinely regrets; it never hurt anyone, but it seeded a bad road.
- **The needle scene — Oregon curtain**: buys the box, rigs the works, tries a poke, says no, puts it in the bin/away ("up my pooper" — still never used a needle, the line set at 15 held), calls home: "I have to come home." The drive home is an epic of its own — to develop.
- **Treatment critique**: asked for what would actually work many times; kept getting 12-step rehab ("bullshit"). 12-step = spiritual framework dressed as medicine; wrong tool for someone wired like this. **Suboxone works once accessed** — the systemic point made without dwelling in the using years.
- **Marine friend + meth**: frame-breaker — Building 7, Tesla, depleted uranium shells in Iraq/Afghanistan, ceremonial magic / language-as-spellcasting, binary code as a limiting structure, unlimited energy. Broke the NPR-liberal frame (valuable) while opening a door with a specific political utility. Then the "step deeper" guy — the recruitment ladder, seen for what it was: "I could have been an asset." Silk Road / Bitcoin awareness early (the pizza order, laughing; loved the idea, was chasing the dragon). Never took the door.
- **Time-slip incidents** (recurring, from different eras): locked into a movie theater after a screening; woke up in a stranger's family's house; the weird "intelligence-feeling" guy who couldn't leave (pool, drinks, "pills that were some crazy new opiate you wouldn't know of" — clearly not opiates), and the next morning **all four car doors open, trunk popped**, car locked, no stash, guy never in the car. Reads like a protocol run on a certain type: intelligent, politically awake, outside mainstream systems, comfortable in underground economies, good at reading rooms, with risk tolerance. Not conspiracy-jack-off — a lived, informed pattern of approach opportunities.
- 2008 crash; Art Institute debt (graduated 2006; the promise made to creative kids broke visibly).

Volume 3 — Act Three: The Slip (the return / time mechanics / resolution) — ~80–120 pp

- Future JD slides back and forth in time (the mechanism = the narrator's real cognition). Sees the slop and deterioration of systems and the collective understanding of what the world is and could be.
- Purpose: help young JD survive the tunnel, plant seeds, and warn about what's coming (capitalism + tech oligarchy). Not necessarily preventing the pain — some of it has to happen; showing it's survivable is the gift.
- **The Boomer Matrix**: not extraction — a merciful, dignified off-ramp. Boomers get a comfortable simulation of the world they understand ("line go up," capitalism humming) while the rest of us build the **Buckminster Fuller abundance world** in the margins with the real tools. Satire + sincerity, the tonal core of the third act.
- **Closing question for the reader**: if you could go back knowing everything (the 15, the tunnel, the debt, relationships that didn't survive) — would you change the path? Or was the path, exactly as broken as it was, the only one that produces someone who can see what you see?

Core Characters
- **JD** — b. 1984, Sioux Falls. Undiagnosed neurodivergent, only child, hyperreal recall, chameleon/masks, high above-average intelligence + creative drive. Voice: funny, politically awake, not bitter, clear-eyed about damage.
- **Dad** — not a villain, a cautionary portrait. Republican, Toastmasters, MLM, The Secret, NLP. Wanted an audience for his big-brain thoughts; found acting. Army dropout (yuk), kids by 22, divorce, 18-year-old hair-school mom. Same model of human as JD — different inputs, different output. Doesn't believe in therapy.
- **Mom** — 18, hair school. Mostly offscreen for now; present as context.
- **Spencer** — Manson shirt, Skankin' Pickle, Asian Man Records. The fork.
- **Corey** — rich psycho. Clothesline, window, mushroom swap, boarding school. Parents CIA-adjacent in the way they lived. The mother and father are warm presence despite it all.
- **Jeff** — trip buddy, grow-kit partner, Amsterdam "condom on with her mouth dude."
- **Nicole / Nick** — guitarist, first love, EMA's (Erica M. Anderson's) younger sister; drums on EMA tours later. Reconnected in Seattle, Portland years, the fizzle, the transition discovered years later. **Ethics note in-story**: Nick's story is Nick's to tell — the distance, the real-but-incomplete connection, the recontextualization are JD's to write; the *reason* stays unnamed. Anonymization is hard because of EMA; change names, imply, never narrate Nick's interior.
- **Andrew** — drummer, CF, OD death = Act Two hinge. Loved like a brother.
- **Adam** — Sioux Falls→Seattle, "dude I got heroin," the scammer, dies ~2 years after Andrew.
- **Marine friend** — meth years, frame-breaker, underbelly education.
- **The "step deeper" guy** — the escalation. See-through, never walked through.
- **The capsule guy** — can't-leave, car doors. Runs it, vanishes.
- **Rick Steves** — fictionalized; wholesome voice of the mainstream narrative + the road not taken. Cheerfully unaware he's a symbol.
- **Future JD** — older self, the slip mechanic; visuals to develop.

Themes
- **Information as survival** — Erowid, tape trading, message boards, harm reduction. Kids with real information make different choices. JD vs. Adam: not moral superiority, *access*.
- **Class as invisible infrastructure** — Corey's Olympic tickets, Nicole's lawyer dad, Adam's family money as a floor that paradoxically makes the fall worse, Art Institute debt as the false promise.
- **Culture as transmission** — the disc golf course, tape network, Saddle Creek regional identity, basement-show circuit as alternative infrastructure.
- **Forks in the road** — Spencer's shirt. If dad had won, none of this.
- **Systems failure** — treatment industry, education, media narrative, promised-to-your-generation economy.
- **The chameleon who reads the sale** — sees the offers (recruitment, scams, the matrix) and chooses doors deliberately.

Production Math & Format
- Comparable benchmarks: Blankets (592 pp), Maus (296), Fun Home (232), Persepolis (160).
- Realistic total: **~350–450 pages** → three volumes (~180 / ~150 / ~120).
- Artist: 1–2 finished pages/day full-time → ~400 working days (18–24 months) at this complexity.
- Page rate: ~$100–300/page indie literary artist. $150 × 400 = **$60k art** before printing, design, editing.
- **Release sequence**: (1) voice-memo everything now, raw, unedited — the archive and first draft simultaneously; (2) Substack serialization in JD's own voice to build 500–1,000 subscribers *before* needing them; (3) artist collab → 8–10 proof-of-concept pages → Kickstarter Volume 1; (4) V1 (~180 pp) funds V2; publisher interest or second campaign after V1.
- **Kickstarter roughs**: V1 art at $150×180 = ~$27k; + lettering, design, printing, fulfillment, platform fees → realistic goal range ~$40–55k; tiered rewards (digital, print, original art, name-in-thanks, SK family portrait cameo). Volume 1 must be self-contained.
- **Podcast option**: genuinely viable given audio-engineering background; 2009's "podcasts will never make money" joke lands differently now.
- **Why graphic novel + not YouTube**: story doesn't fit YouTube chunks; GN gives room for interior monologue + nonlinear time. Weird-cartoon-sitcom framing (Daria → Liquid Television) possible for a spinoff register.

Artist Outreach Steps
- **Where to look**: Kickstarter comics/graphic-novel category alumni; Sequential Artists Workshop; small-press communities (SPX, MOCCA, LIBCAF, CAKE Chicago), Minneapolis/Upper-Midwest comics scene (an advantage — regionally closer); artist alleys at cons; indie GN creators on Substack/Bluesky.
- **What to bring**: this bible, 4–8 reference photos of Sioux Falls today (Tut Hill, neighborhoods, ACE-era streetscapes, golden-hour light), a voice memo of one key scene, sample panels, budget + schedule page, kill-fee / flat-page-rate terms (no "exposure" deals — killed by deal incentives).
- **The test**: one sample page — clothesline or Amsterdam smash cut or glass house — and see whether their instincts on *tonal range* match (grit + psychedelia + grief + humor in one body of work).
- **Reference-photo mission (JD, no cost)**: Tut Hill Park, disc golf shots, the ACE building if still standing, middle-school era geography, the neighborhood where Corey's house was, longboard-able hills, airport/highway framing for departures, anything 90s getting demolished.

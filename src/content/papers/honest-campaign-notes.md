These are the working notes from the Opendoors exercise — the numbers that survived, and the ones that didn't.

## The fee stack nobody quotes

Kickstarter takes **5%** of funds raised, payment processors take **~3–5%**, and rewards that ship add fulfillment + shipping on top. For a $29 product, the fully-loaded cost per unit — product, shipping to backer, platform, processing, contingency — can consume half the pledge. [fact: Kickstarter fee structure; estimate: typical processing]

## The real bottleneck

Campaign success correlates more with **pre-launch audience** than with the product. The benchmark used here: 1,000+ waitlist emails before launch day. A great page converts warm traffic; it cannot create it.

## What the mock taught me

Pricing a physical good honestly means pricing it *after* fees, not before. If the per-unit margin after all-in costs is under ~40%, one fulfillment mishap turns the campaign into a donation to the backers.

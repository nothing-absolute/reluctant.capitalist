## THE RELUCTANT CAPITALIST
**Draft: The Rent-Seeking Trap (And How to Walk Away)**

There is a concept in economics that completely changed how I view the world, and more importantly, how I viewed my family's history. It’s called "rent-seeking."

In classical economics, value is created when you produce something new or improve something that exists. You build a chair, you write software, you farm a crop. You add value to the system, and you are compensated for it.

Rent-seeking is the opposite. Rent-seeking is when an entity extracts wealth from a system without adding any value to it. Think of a toll booth placed on a road that the toll collector didn't build and doesn't maintain. They just stand there, demanding a cut of the traffic.

When I was at Portland Community College, sitting in an economics 101 class, the professor put the definition of rent-seeking on the whiteboard. I stared at it for the entire hour. Because suddenly, the last 15 years of my life made sense.

My dad wasn't an entrepreneur, though he desperately wanted to be. He was captured by a system of ultimate rent-seekers: the Multi-Level Marketing industry.

MLMs do not create value. They take a cheap commodity (a vitamin, a shampoo, a digital course) and inflate its price by 500% to pay a sprawling, mathematically doomed hierarchy. But the real rent-seeking isn't even the product. The real rent-seeking is the *culture of failure*. 

When a distributor inevitably fails to sell the overpriced commodity to their friends, the system swoops in. They sell the distributor a $500 mindset coaching seminar. They sell them a $1,200 ticket to the annual convention to "recharge their energy." They sell them $50/month app subscriptions to track their non-existent leads. 

The system extracts rent on the failure that the system itself engineered. 

It is a toll booth on human desperation.

### The Escape Hatch

Once you see rent-seeking, you see it everywhere. You see it in private equity firms gutting local newspapers. You see it in subscription models for heated car seats. You see it in "Master Resell Rights" courses where the only product is the right to sell the course to the next person.

But you also realize what the alternative is. 

If rent-seeking is extraction without creation, the antidote is creation without extraction. 

This is why maker culture, open-source software, and DIY punk communities feel so profoundly different. They operate on a completely different economic engine. In a maker space, if you know how to weld, you teach the person next to you. In an open-source project, you write code and give it away so the entire system gets stronger. 

These aren't just hobbies. They are structural rebellions against a rent-seeking economy. 

Humans are fundamentally communal creatures. When our basic needs are met, our default state is not to extract from our neighbors—it is to contribute to our communities, progress our species, and preserve our natural systems. 

The capitalist mind virus tells us that the toll booth is the only way to survive. The machine wants you to believe there is no other way. 

But there is. You just have to be willing to build it.

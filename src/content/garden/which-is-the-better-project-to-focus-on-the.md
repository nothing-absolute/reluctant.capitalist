This is a great question. You are looking at two very different projects with completely different target audiences, manufacturing complexities, and market dynamics. 

Here is my analysis on which project to focus on, followed by a breakdown of Kickstarter vs. Indiegogo.

### 1. Minimal Smartphone (Lumina Phone & Lumina Kids)
This project taps into a growing, mainstream cultural movement: digital minimalism and children's digital safety. 

**Pros:**
* **Massive Potential Market:** Almost every parent is worried about screen time, and a growing number of adults want to disconnect. The Total Addressable Market (TAM) is huge.
* **High Average Order Value:** Based on our projections, an average pledge of ~$300 makes it easier to hit large funding milestones quickly.
* **Viral Potential:** Digital detox and child safety are highly shareable topics on social media, making organic marketing much easier.
* **Smart Strategy:** By utilizing off-the-shelf hardware and focusing on a custom, locked-down OS for the initial tiers, you significantly reduce the risk compared to building a phone from scratch.

**Cons:**
* **High Expectations:** Consumer electronics have a very high bar for UI/UX, battery life, and customer support. 
* **Supply Chain Complexity:** Even with off-the-shelf hardware, managing bulk purchasing, flashing the OS, packaging, and global logistics is a massive undertaking.
* **Fierce Competition:** You are competing with established players like the Light Phone, Mudita, and even Apple/Google's built-in parental controls.

### 2. BYO SIM Boat Monitor (BilgeGuard)
This project is a classic, high-utility niche hardware product. It solves a very specific, expensive problem (boats sinking or batteries dying at the marina).

**Pros:**
* **High Willingness to Pay:** Boat owners are accustomed to paying a premium for marine electronics. A reliable bilge/battery monitor saves them thousands of dollars, making it an easy sell.
* **Simpler Hardware & Manufacturing:** An ESP32-based system (like the Heltec LoRa) with standard marine sensors is much easier to source, assemble, and support than a smartphone. 
* **Strong Community:** The DIY/Maker marine community (like OpenPlotter users) is incredibly engaged and supportive of open-source/hackable hardware.
* **Software is Ready:** We've already mapped out a highly premium, interactive React simulator that serves as a massive trust-builder for potential backers.

**Cons:**
* **Smaller Market:** The audience is restricted to boat owners who are technically inclined enough to install a BYO SIM system (though this can be mitigated by offering a pre-configured SIM option).
* **Lower Viral Potential:** It is a utility product, meaning it's less likely to get picked up by mainstream tech press compared to a minimalist phone.

### The Verdict: Which to focus on?
**I recommend focusing on the BYO SIM Boat Monitor (BilgeGuard) first.** 

Why? Because it is the perfect **"stepping stone"** project. 
Launching a hardware product is incredibly difficult. The Boat Monitor has lower manufacturing risk, a highly targeted audience, and solves a painful problem for a community with disposable income. Successfully funding and delivering the BilgeGuard will teach you the logistics of manufacturing, fulfillment, and customer support. You can then take that track record of success (and the capital you raise) and apply it to the much more ambitious Minimalist Smartphone project down the line.

### Kickstarter vs. Indiegogo: Which is better for your hardware?

Both platforms are excellent for hardware, but they have distinct differences in culture, rules, and post-campaign support.

#### Kickstarter
* **Funding Model:** All-or-nothing. If you don't hit your goal, you get nothing, and backers aren't charged. This creates urgency and protects you from having to fulfill orders without enough capital.
* **The Audience:** Kickstarter has a larger organic user base. "Serial backers" browse Kickstarter looking for cool projects to support.
* **The Rules (Important for Hardware):** Kickstarter is very strict about hardware. You **must** show a working prototype. You cannot use photorealistic renders to misrepresent a product's current state of development. 
* **Best For:** Building brand legitimacy, tapping into an organic community of early adopters, and projects that need a strict threshold of capital to afford tooling/manufacturing.

#### Indiegogo
* **Funding Model:** Offers both "All-or-nothing" and "Flexible" funding (you keep what you raise, even if you miss the goal). *Note: Flexible funding for hardware is very risky if you need a minimum volume to pay for manufacturing.*
* **The Audience:** Slightly more tech and gadget-focused, but relies heavier on you bringing your own external traffic via ads or email lists.
* **The Rules:** Generally more lenient than Kickstarter regarding the state of prototypes and manufacturing. 
* **Post-Campaign (InDemand):** This is Indiegogo's killer feature. After your campaign ends, you can seamlessly transition into "InDemand" mode, allowing you to continue accepting pre-orders on the platform while you manufacture the product. (Many successful Kickstarter campaigns actually move over to Indiegogo InDemand after their Kickstarter ends to keep capturing sales).
* **Best For:** Projects that already have their own large email list/marketing engine, teams that want built-in tracking pixels for Facebook/Google Ads (Kickstarter's pixel integration is notoriously clunky), and leveraging the InDemand pre-order phase.

**Recommendation on Platform:** 
For the **Boat Monitor**, use **Kickstarter**. The maker/DIY community lives on Kickstarter, and the all-or-nothing model ensures you can afford the initial PCB/component order.
For the **Minimalist Smartphone**, **Indiegogo** might be the better fit due to their robust ad-tracking integrations (since a consumer phone will require heavy paid advertising to scale) and the InDemand feature to keep pre-orders flowing while you navigate the complex OS-flashing supply chain.

I have spent most of my life being good at making things and bad at the part where you convince someone to pay for them. For a long time I treated that as a character flaw to apologize for. This site is the opposite move: an attempt to make the *apology itself* the product.

## The tension

The name says it in two words. I am reluctant to be a capitalist — the word carries everything I find unlovely: persuasion, optimization, the constant arithmetic of who gets what. And yet: ideas don't ship themselves. Materials cost money. Rent is not a metaphor.

So here is the position I am staking out:

- **Ideas are seeds, not assets.** The point of an idea is that someone carries it forward. Hoarding one is like holding a seed in your fist and calling it a garden.
- **Shipping is a skill, like welding or writing.** It can be learned, it can be practiced, and it is worth more than most ideas.
- **Honest economics beats clever narratives.** I would rather tell you what a thing costs and why than sell you a story about it.
- **Public is a discipline.** If I write the goals down, they get harder to abandon quietly. If I show the numbers, they get harder to fudge.

## The reluctant part is the point

I am not building this site to become a better salesman. I am building it so I never have to become a salesman at all. If the work is honest and the receipts are public, the money can show up on its own — slowly, in the right amounts, from people who wanted the thing, not the pitch.

That is the whole experiment. The papers, projects, comics, and garden notes on this site are the lab notes.

**21:37 - KARR Car Alarm Vulnerability Business Opportunity**
From WIRED (Jul 21, 2026, Andy Greenberg): UCSD researchers found the KARR aftermarket car alarm (installed by dealers in ~2M+ US vehicles, half without buyer consent) has a universal Bluetooth auth key — any nearby hacker can unlock cars, kill ignition (roadside paralysis), track them via WiGLE, and run group "mayhem" attacks. Vendor (Acrisure/SWDS) shipped a free firmware patch Jul 2026 (~18 months after disclosure). Key business angle: the PATCH is free + DIY via the KARR app — so the real business is DETECTION (half of owners don't know they have it), convenience patching, and physical REMOVAL (durable value: ends beaconing/tracking/attack surface forever). Startup: mobile tech, ~$15–30k (tools, insurance, CA BAR licensing), pricing detection $0–30 / patch $50–100 / removal $150–300. Highest device density: Southern California. Go-to-market: ride the Defcon/Usenix news wave, SEO, dealer + used-lot + fleet remediation contracts. Adjacent expansion: generalize to vehicle privacy/security audits for hidden dealer GPS trackers (big subprime-lending issue) and FTC add-on rule compliance. Time-limited window, but removal demand persists.

**21:52 - KARR Business Expansion: Financial Model, B2B, Marketing + Long-Term Research**
Deep-dive on the KARR remediation business (from prior capture). Key additions:
- UCSD study site (bleauto.sysnet.ucsd.edu): analyzed SIX aftermarket BLE systems (KARR, CarLink, Rockledge, DS4, EvoStart, Linkr); THREE vulnerable (KARR, CarLink, Rockledge). 2.2M+ vehicles. Rockledge (a car-security AND insurance co) vulnerable via replay, disclosure open. So it's a whole device CLASS, not one vendor.
- Financial model: startup $15–35k (tools $3–8k, van $5–15k, insurance $2–5k, marketing $1–3k, buffer). Unit economics: scan $25–50, patch $60–100 (20–30 min), removal $150–300 (1–2 hrs), audit $200–400; 70–80% gross margin; 1 tech = 4–6 jobs/day. Ramp: m1–3 $4–6k/mo, m4–6 $10–14k, m7–12 $20–30k (2 techs + B2B). Break-even ~$5k/mo fixed ≈ 35–40 jobs. B2B: dealer lot audit $20–40/car, bulk removal $120–200/car, fleet subscription $50–100/veh/yr.
- Repair-shop partnership: shops scan every car during inspection (free 5-min Bluetooth scan), offer remediation as add-on, split 50/50 or referral fee; shop does 10 cars/day → 10% have devices → 30% opt-in → ~3 jobs/day. Positions shops as tech-forward, zero training cost.
- Long-term market (research): (1) SIDs/GPS kill switches in subprime loans — up to 70% of subprime vehicles; USASF/CFPB: 7,523 wrongful disables, $32.6M relief + $10M penalty; devices left in after payoff, unregulated by NHTSA. (2) GPS trackers: SinoTrack 6M+ devices w/ default passwords (CVE-2025-5484/5485, unpatched), MiCODUS, Meitrack CVE-2025-51643. (3) Keyless relay attacks: 85% of keyless cars vulnerable (ADAC), kits $100–1000, <1 min theft; OBD hacks tied to CA theft rates (Mercury/NICB). (4) Connected-car app hacks: Subaru STARLINK admin takeover (Sam Curry), Pandora/Viper smart alarms IDOR (2019). (5) Aftermarket remote start CarLinkBT hardcoded XXTEA key (VerSprite 2018, unpatched, no OTA).
- Durable business angles: hidden-device detection/removal (trackers + SIDs + alarms), "digital safety inspection"/certification product (like Carfax for hidden devices, data product for dealers/auctions/insurers), keyless anti-theft hardening (Faraday + ghost immobilizers + UWB), fleet telematics security audits (CISA advisory playbook), dealer FTC add-on compliance audits, insurance-certified theft protection. KARR is a wedge into a systemic unregulated problem.

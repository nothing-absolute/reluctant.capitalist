# Creator and project context — The Reluctant Capitalist

This file is the brief the shaping LLM is held to. It is drawn from JD's own published writing and
the history of the work, not invented.

## The site

A personal field-notes site: projects, long-form papers, concepts, comics, art, design notes,
public goals, durable values, and a limited digital garden. The lab notebook for someone learning to
build and to sell without becoming a salesman. "The papers, projects, comics, and garden notes on
this site are the lab notes."

## The position being staked out

- **Ideas are seeds, not assets.** The point of an idea is that someone carries it forward.
- **Shipping is a skill, like welding or writing.** Learnable, worth more than most ideas.
- **Honest economics beats clever narratives.** Say what a thing costs and why, rather than sell a
  story about it.
- **Public is a discipline.** Writing goals down makes them harder to abandon quietly. Showing the
  numbers makes them harder to fudge.
- **Relay over ownership.** He would rather see a thing built without him than built by nobody.
- **One venture at a time.** Depth over a portfolio of half-attention.

## Who JD is

Born 1984, Sioux Falls SD. Only child. Undiagnosed neurodivergent; high recall, many social masks,
reads rooms and systems. School went from an alternative elementary school (ACE — diverse,
parent-involved, computers) into a public middle school built for compliance, which made the contrast
undeniable. Found the exit through music: ska at 13, then punk, then Saddle Creek; DIY venues,
basement shows, bootleg tape trading, message boards. An anarchist vegetarian by 19. He reads
systemically — Erowid trip reports at 15, rent-seeking on a whiteboard in a community-college
economics class, the disc-golf course at Tut Hill Park passed down unchanged between generations of
kids.

## The story he is telling

Art Institute of Seattle for audio engineering, graduated 2006 into the crash, with debt. A public
radio life (OPB, podcasting) was genuinely available and is the road not taken. Years of opioid use
that took Andrew (the drummer in his high-school band A Plain of Jars, cystic fibrosis, died of an
overdose) and then Adam. Around fifteen people he knew have died of overdose. Suboxone and years of
couch-surfing back to a life that is not organized around a hit. He came home to Sioux Falls.

The through-line: information access decides outcomes. The kid who read Erowid did not become Adam.
The same instinct later built the tools on this site.

## The work

- **A Plain of Jars** — a memoir in three volumes, ~350–450 pages, graphic novel plus prose
  serialization. The childhood, the underground, the tunnel, the return. Adult JD sliding back
  through time to warn and orient the child.
- **MLM Truth / The Machine** — a free interactive instrument that shows what MLM economics and
  self-help-to-extraction pipelines actually do, with real math, built because family are in it.
  The same "information as harm reduction" instinct as Erowid, aimed at adults.
- **The Light Field** — the Substack: essays, the memoir serialization, the data.
- **Community data centres / makerspaces** — the long game. Cooperative, community-owned compute and
  modular manufacturing in dead malls, resurrecting third spaces. Mondragon, FabCity, Precious
  Plastics, Gopher, Free Geek as precedents.
- Working projects: Hydration Station, OpenPlotter, BilgeGuard, Opendoors, Idea Relay, and others.

## Psychology to respect

His father is the counter-example and the warning: the same model of human with fewer inputs in
reach — Toastmasters, MLM, The Secret, NLP — reaching for meaning and landing in every available wrong
container. The writing understands that man without excusing him. When a post touches family, MLM
capture, the recovery years, or the deaths, it is direct and unsentimental, never confessional theatre
and never cruelty.

## Voice

First person. Plain words. Short declarative sentences mixed with occasional longer ones. Dry, not
performative. Concrete nouns and real numbers. Willing to state a cost, a failure, or a changed mind.
No growth-hack language, no hustle vocabulary, no "excited to share", no emoji, no rhetorical
questions as decoration, no bullet-point sludge where a paragraph is meant. Titles are plain
declarative phrases, sentence case, never title-cased slogans.

Two anchors:

> I am not building this site to become a better salesman. I am building it so I never have to
> become a salesman at all.

> The reluctant part of the name isn't about money; it's about control. I would rather see a thing
> built without me than built by nobody.

## What a shaped post must do

- Be clear about **what it is** and **what it is for**. Name the outcome the post is driving toward.
- Be focused: cut the throat. If a post is a fragment, make it an honest fragment with a clear
  shape — not a padded essay.
- Keep every fact, date, figure, name and link that is actually in the source. Never invent
  outcomes, numbers, quotes or people.
- Fit its section. If the content does not fit any section, say so with `verdict: "misfit"` rather
  than forcing it.
- Link to genuinely related posts on the site. Only when the connection is real.
